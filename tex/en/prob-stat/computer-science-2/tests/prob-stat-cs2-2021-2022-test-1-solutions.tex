\section*{Problem 1}
Let I denote the ordinary six-sided die. For an announced value $k\le6$,
\[\P(I\mid\text{announced }k)=\frac{\frac16\cdot\frac12}{\frac16\cdot\frac12+\frac12\cdot\frac18}=\frac1{1+3/4}=\frac47.\]
The probability is zero otherwise.
\section*{Problem 2}
The attending students are represented by the counts
\[\text{Year 1: }\frac45\cdot600=480,\qquad\text{Year 2: }\frac35\cdot400=240,\qquad\text{Year 3: }\frac14\cdot200=50.\]
The first calculation in the notes uses
\[\frac{50(480+240)}{480\cdot240+480\cdot50+240\cdot50}.\]
A more formal approach conditions on attendance:
\[\P(\text{Year 1}\mid\text{attends})=
\frac{\frac45\frac{600}{1200}}{\frac45\frac{600}{1200}+\frac35\frac{400}{1200}+\frac14\frac{200}{1200}}=\frac{48}{77},\]
\[\P(\text{Year 2}\mid\text{attends})=\frac{24}{77},\qquad
\P(\text{Year 3}\mid\text{attends})=\frac5{77}.\]
The desired conditional probability is that the higher-year student is in Year 3, given attendance and different years. The notes combine the Year 3 / Year 1 and Year 3 / Year 2 cases in the numerator, and add the Year 1 / Year 2 case in the denominator.

\section*{Problem 4}
\subsection*{(a) Direct transmission}
\[\P(\text{no errors})=(1-p)^8,\qquad\E[Bin(8,1-p)]=8(1-p).\]
\subsection*{(b) Three intermediate servers}
The message travels through four transmissions, so a bit is received correctly after zero, two or four errors. Thus
\[\P(\text{bit }i\text{ is correct})=(1-p)^4+\binom42(1-p)^2p^2+p^4=:q.\]
Consequently,
\[\P(\text{the message is correct})=q^8,\qquad\E[Bin(8,q)]=8q.\]
For $2n$ transmissions,
\[q=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom{2n}{2k}p^{2k}(1-p)^{2n-2k}.\]
Using
\[\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{2n-k}=1\]
and
\[\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{2n-k}(-1)^k=(p-(1-p))^{2n},\]
the notes conclude
\[2q=1+(2p-1)^{2n},\qquad q\longrightarrow\frac12\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,\quad |2p-1|<1.\]
